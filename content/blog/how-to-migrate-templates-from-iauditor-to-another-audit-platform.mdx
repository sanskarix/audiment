## How Do You Export Your Checklists and Data Out of SafetyCulture?

**You export checklists and data out of SafetyCulture by accessing the web platform as an administrator, selecting the Template Builder, choosing 'Export Template' in CSV or JSON format, and running bulk data exports for your historical audit records in CSV and PDF formats.**

To secure your operational assets before closing or downgrading your SafetyCulture workspace:

### 1. Exporting Active Templates
1. Log into the SafetyCulture web app with admin privileges.
2. Navigate to the **Templates** dashboard.
3. Open each active inspection template.
4. Click the three dots (More Options) in the top-right corner and select **Export**.
5. Choose **CSV** format for flat question-and-answer structures, or **JSON** if you have complex nested conditional branches.

### 2. Archiving Historical Inspection Reports
Never leave your historical audit records behind. In SafetyCulture:
* Navigate to **Audits / Inspections**.
* Apply date filters to encompass your full operational history (e.g., past 24-36 months).
* Select **Export to CSV** to retain all tabular score data, timestamps, user IDs, and location names.
* Use the bulk PDF export feature to download completed audit reports to an external secure cloud repository (AWS S3 or Google Drive) for regulatory retention.

## Why Should You Refactor and Prune Templates During Migration?

**You should refactor and prune templates during migration because legacy checklists in iAuditor frequently suffer from template bloat, accumulating subjective 1-to-5 ratings, duplicate questions, and obsolete procedures that slow down inspections and encourage pencil-whipping.**

Over time, different department heads add pet questions to an audit template. An opening checklist that started at 25 questions gradually swells to 120 items, forcing store managers to spend 45 minutes tapping screens rather than supervising their teams.

Migration is the ideal moment to prune your checklists using the "Ruthless Audit Rubric":

| Legacy iAuditor Question Format | Why It Fails | Modern Proof-Based Replacement |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| *Rate kitchen cleanliness from 1 to 5 stars* | Completely subjective; Auditor A gives a 4, Auditor B gives a 2 for identical conditions. | *Are all stainless steel prep counters sanitised with zero food residue? (Yes / No + Photo)* |
| *Are staff following all grooming policies?* | Too broad; ignores specific health code requirements like hairnets or beard restraints. | *Are 100% of kitchen food handlers wearing approved hair restraints and clean aprons? (Yes / No)* |
| *What is the walk-in temperature? (Text box)* | Staff type numbers without checking the thermometer or guess from memory. | *Walk-in cooler temperature probe readout (Numeric range 0-4°C + Live photo of display)* |

Pruning subjective bloat cuts average inspection times by 35% while dramatically increasing the operational reliability of the data collected.

## How Do You Handle Complex Conditional Logic and Multiple-Choice Branches?

**Handle complex conditional logic by simplifying multi-layered nested trees into streamlined binary gates that trigger secondary questions or mandatory photo fields only when a non-conformance is identified.**

In iAuditor, templates often contain four levels of nested logic (e.g., "If No -> If Severity High -> If Correctable Today -> Ask Question B"). In field conditions with spotty network connectivity or older mobile hardware, deeply nested conditional trees cause mobile apps to lag and crash.

Best practices for rebuilding conditional logic in your new audit engine:

1. **Trigger Corrective Action on "No":** Structure questions so that "Yes" represents standard compliance and moves immediately to the next item. Selecting "No" instantly expands:
   * A mandatory live camera viewfinder (gallery disabled).
   * A root-cause defect dropdown (e.g., Equipment Malfunction, Training Gap, Missing Stock).
   * An auto-generated Corrective and Preventive Action (CAPA) ticket.
2. **Flatten Category Trees:** Group questions by physical store zones (e.g., Exterior, Dining Room, Kitchen Line, Storage, Restrooms) rather than arbitrary departmental silos. This allows the auditor to walk a natural, continuous physical circuit around the premises.

## How Do You Transition Field Auditors and Store Managers Without Disruption?

**Transition field teams without disruption by running a two-week parallel pilot across 10% of your outlets, holding a 30-minute mobile training session, and establishing a single migration coordinator to resolve field queries in real time.**

Resistance to software change usually stems from fear of complicated new interfaces. Ensure smooth operational adoption with this three-step rollout plan:

* **Phase 1: Parallel Pilot (Days 1-7):** Select five representative store locations. Have area managers run inspections using both the old iAuditor template and the new platform side by side. Compare speed, UI responsiveness, and photo capture reliability.
* **Phase 2: Training & Browser-First Access (Days 8-10):** Send browser-based direct links to store managers. Modern platforms like Audiment require zero app store downloads to complete daily checklists, allowing frontline staff to begin logging checks immediately on any mobile browser.
* **Phase 3: Hard Cutover (Day 14):** Announce a definitive switch date. Freeze edits in the old iAuditor workspace, downgrade accounts to free or read-only tiers to prevent dual reporting, and direct all operational dashboards to the new central system.
