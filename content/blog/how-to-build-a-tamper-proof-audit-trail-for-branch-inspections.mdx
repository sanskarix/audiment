## Why do spreadsheets and paper logs fail statutory audit trail standards?

> **Spreadsheets and paper logs fail statutory standards because they lack change attribution, allow retroactive modifications without version logs, cannot verify physical user location, and permit unverified image uploads from external sources.**

Regulators and court examiners reject manual inspection records during forensic compliance investigations due to four fatal flaws:

1. **No verifiable user attribution:** Paper sign-offs can be forged by colleagues; Excel files can be modified by anyone with file permissions without logging who made the alteration.
2. **Retroactive backdating:** Spreadsheets permit users to backdate inspection entries to yesterday or last week without leaving an immutable audit log.
3. **Absence of spatial proof:** Paper forms and spreadsheets provide zero evidence confirming the auditor was physically at the branch rather than working remotely.
4. **Metadata stripping:** Photos pasted into Word documents or spreadsheets lose their original Exif metadata (focal length, GPS, original camera timestamp), rendering them legally inadmissible.

## Frequently Asked Questions

<details>
<summary><strong>Can an enterprise administrator delete or modify a completed audit report?</strong></summary>
<p>No. To preserve regulatory integrity, completed audits are stored in an append-only state. If an administrative adjustment is legally required, the system creates an addendum record linked to the original file, documenting the administrator ID, exact timestamp, and justification without altering the historical entry.</p>

<details>
<summary><strong>How does the system handle branch inspections in basements with zero GPS reception?</strong></summary>
<p>When satellite GPS signals are unavailable inside shielded basements or vaults, the software captures the initial GPS verification at the building entrance and verifies physical presence in the basement using physical QR code or NFC chip scans mounted directly on facility equipment.</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>Are cryptographically signed digital inspection dossiers admissible in court?</strong></summary>
<p>Yes. Digital records that incorporate unalterable timestamps, device identifiers, GPS verification, and SHA-256 cryptographic hashes meet statutory requirements for electronic evidence under global legal frameworks (such as the US Federal Rules of Evidence and the Indian Information Technology Act Section 65B).</p>
</details>

<details>
<summary><strong>How does the platform prevent emulators and automated robotic script submissions?</strong></summary>
<p>Audiment validates device hardware attestation using Google Play Integrity API on Android and DeviceCheck on iOS. Virtual machines, cloud emulators, and rooted debug environments fail hardware attestation, permanently blocking the app from generating audit payloads.</p>
</details>

<summary><strong>Can branch managers contest an audit report without corrupting the historical record?</strong></summary>
<p>Yes. Branch managers can initiate an on-screen dispute within 48 hours of audit submission. The dispute attaches counter-evidence and manager commentary to the case file without overwriting the inspector's original scores or captured photographs, preserving the complete chain of custody.</p>
</details>
