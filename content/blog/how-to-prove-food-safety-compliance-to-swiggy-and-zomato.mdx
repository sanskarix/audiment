## How Do Swiggy and Zomato Audit Cloud Kitchen Partners?

**Swiggy and Zomato audit cloud kitchen partners by validating FSSAI licence documentation on partner portals, commissioning third-party food safety auditing agencies to conduct unannounced on-site kitchen inspections, and tracking customer food safety dispute tickets to trigger targeted operational reviews.**

Aggregator compliance operates across three distinct evaluation channels:

1. **Mandatory Portal Documentation:** Aggregators require cloud kitchen partners to upload valid FSSAI State or Central licences matching their exact physical kitchen address. Failure to renew or provide updated food category approvals leads to automated listing suspensions.
2. **Empaneled Third-Party Kitchen Audits:** Both platforms partner with FSSAI-empaneled inspection agencies to conduct unannounced physical audits of cloud kitchens. These auditors evaluate food contact surface sanitation, kitchen ventilation, waste disposal, and pest barriers.
3. **Customer Complaint Thresholds:** Aggregator algorithms continuously monitor customer review keywords (such as "stale," "smell," "raw," or "hair found"). If an outlet crosses a specific dispute threshold, the platform dispatches a mandatory inspection and temporarily disables the kitchen's delivery visibility.
4. **Hygiene Rating Badging:** Outlets that achieve high scores during third-party hygiene audits earn prominent "Hygiene Verified" or "FSSAI 4/5 Star" badges on the consumer app, directly boosting customer click-through and order conversion rates.

## How to Respond to an Aggregator Food Safety Warning or De-Listing

**Respond to an aggregator food safety warning or de-listing by conducting an immediate internal audit, generating an incident investigation report with photo proof of corrective actions, and submitting the remediation dossier to the partner compliance desk within 24 hours.**

When an aggregator flags a serious hygiene complaint, kitchen operators must take immediate, documented action to restore their listing:

```
[ Aggregator Flags Customer Complaint or Failed Audit ]
                          ↓
[ Kitchen Manager Conducts Emergency Internal Inspection ]
  • Test cold storage temperatures & cooking oil TPM
  • Inspect batch prep labels & packaging seal stocks
                          ↓
[ Auto-Generate Corrective Action (CAPA) Report ]
  • Log technician repair receipts for any cooling drift
  • Discard suspected ingredient batches with photo proof
  • Complete deep sanitization of food contact surfaces
                          ↓
[ Submit Verification Dossier to Aggregator Support ]
  • Export PDF audit report containing timestamps & photos
  • Attach valid FSSAI, water test, and pest control slips
                          ↓
[ Aggregator Re-Evaluates & Re-Activates Listing ]
```

1. **Isolate the Specific Batch:** Review the customer's order ticket timestamp. Cross-reference your digital shift checklist to verify which staff were on duty, what the walk-in temperature was during prep, and when that batch of protein or sauce was prepped.
2. **Execute Photo-Verified CAPA:** If the complaint was caused by an equipment defect (such as an undercounter fridge running warm), upload an invoice from a refrigeration technician and a live photo of the digital thermometer showing restored temperatures (under 4°C).
3. **Submit the Full Digital Trail:** Submit the completed CAPA report alongside your FSSAI licence and pest control slip directly to your Swiggy or Zomato Partner Account Manager. Providing an organized, photo-verified compliance record demonstrates proactive management and dramatically speeds up account reinstatement.

## How Tamper-Evident Packaging and Delivery Handovers Protect Your Brand

**Tamper-evident packaging and organized delivery handovers protect your brand by physically preventing delivery riders from opening food containers during transit, eliminating false customer contamination claims, and providing photo-verified proof of proper dispatch.**

Aggregator customer complaints frequently originate during the 30-minute delivery transit rather than inside the kitchen. Operators prevent transit disputes through three physical packaging controls:

- **Security Seals on Delivery Bags:** Apply heavy-duty branded adhesive stickers across the fold of paper delivery bags. If the bag is opened or torn during transit, the broken seal alerts the customer that tampering occurred outside the kitchen.
- **Ventilated, Tamper-Proof Containers:** Use food-grade polypropylene containers with snap-lock lids that cannot be re-sealed once opened. For fried foods, use steam-vented boxes that prevent condensation build-up without exposing food to road dust.
- **Dispatch Station Photos:** Require kitchen dispatchers to take a wide-angle photo of completed delivery bags staged on the dispatch counter during peak rush. The photo provides a timestamped visual record proving that bags left the kitchen sealed, clean, and properly labeled.
