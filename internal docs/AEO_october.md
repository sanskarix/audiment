## 2. Business and product facts (source: audiment.in)

**One-line description (use verbatim wherever the product is introduced):** Audiment is audit management software for multi-location businesses, built to run proof-based audits, verify on-site execution, and track corrective actions to resolution.

**Positioning:** "You can't be at every branch." Audiment targets operators who rely on managers to report honestly and want proof, not paperwork. The site emphasises operational consistency, evidence, and accountability.

**Core capabilities stated on the site:**
- Browser-based, no app download. Auditors open a link on any phone.
- Works offline; responses save locally and sync when back online.
- Mandatory photo or video evidence on questions the admin marks as evidence-required.
- GPS-verified auditor presence through a short geo-tagged video and selfie. The system flags auditors outside the registered GPS range.
- Auto-timestamps on evidence.
- Failed checklist items create corrective action tasks with an owner, a deadline, and a 48-hour SLA for critical failures.
- Photo proof of the fix is required before a corrective action can close.
- Trend alerts, for example when a location scores below threshold or scores poorly on three consecutive audits.
- Weighted scoring and severity levels.
- Reusable audit blueprints, recurring schedules, and surprise audits (hidden from the manager until the auditor starts).
- Pre-built FSSAI hygiene and safety templates.
- One-click PDF compliance reports with photos, scores, and corrective action history.
- Role-based access: admins see all locations, managers see their branches, auditors see their assigned audits.
- Data separated per organisation at the database level, with automated daily backups.
- Bluetooth temperature probe sync and threshold breach alerts appear in the food safety module.

**Pricing and trial:** Not published on the site. The only visible call to action is "Book a call" or "Book a Demo" (a 20-minute operational walkthrough). Do not state prices, contract terms, or a free trial unless the human confirms them.

**Customer evidence on the site:** Logos (Aster DM Healthcare, Ayatana Resorts, Best Western, Motherhood Hospitals, Tom & Chee, VRL Logistics) and testimonials from named people. Treat all of these as unverified. Do not quote customer numbers such as "60% to 95%" or "5 days to 48 hours" in a blog unless the human confirms the source and permission. Where a case study is used, attribute it and label it as the customer's experience.

**Claims to verify before use:** free trial, multi-language support, white-label or consultant options, data hosting location, the 48-hour SLA as a configurable setting, and any integration (ERP, POS, WhatsApp).

**Competitors named on the site:** SafetyCulture (iAuditor), GoAudits, Jolt, Zenput, and generic checklist apps. Comparison pages exist for each. Blog comparisons must be factual, cover what each competitor does well, and include an honest "where Audiment is not the right fit" section.

**Market focus:** The site and directory describe a focus on South and Southeast Asia, with a strong India and FSSAI angle.

## 3. Audience (ICP) summary

The register groups posts by ICP. In brief:

- **Operators of multi-outlet food businesses** (ICP 1 to 5, 29): QSR chains, restaurant and café chains, cloud kitchens, bakery and dessert chains. Pain: managers filling checklists without doing them, corrective actions that never close, compliance records scattered across WhatsApp.
- **Food safety and quality owners** (ICP 4): need FSSAI-ready records, timestamped evidence, and HACCP-style checklists.
- **Franchisors and franchisees** (ICP 6, 7, 37): need objective, evidence-backed brand compliance, and franchisees need a way to prepare for and answer audits.
- **Retail, grocery, pharmacy, and brand retail** (ICP 8 to 11): visual merchandising, expiry and cold chain, store presentation, and paper replacement.
- **Hospitality** (ICP 12, 13): housekeeping, maintenance tickets, offline resort inspections, property dashboards.
- **Healthcare and clinics** (ICP 10, 14, 15): infection control, biomedical waste, NABH records. This is regulated content; cite sources.
- **Logistics, cold chain, and manufacturing** (ICP 16 to 18): safety walks, SLA-driven fixes, EHS findings, quality audits.
- **Operations and area managers** (ICP 28, 33): time per audit, compiling reports, follow-up, staff training, and fatigue.
- **Buyers and evaluators** (ICP 30 to 32, 34 to 35, 39): cost, ROI, data security, RFPs, pilots, switching from SafetyCulture, GoAudits, Zenput, Jolt, or Excel.
- **Feature-led buyers** (ICP 40): people who already know the capability they need (auto-created tasks, geo-verification, offline mode).

**Deprioritised verticals:** ICP 20 to 24 (petrol pumps, dealerships, gyms and salons, schools, banks and NBFCs, cinemas) have little or no supporting search data. Mark these HOLD unless new data appears. Consultant and outsourced-audit content (ICP 26) falls into a research exclusion category, so keep it narrow and product-led.

## 5. Research protocol (run before writing each post)

For each post, the agent should:

1. **Check the live site.** Confirm the target slug is not already live, and check whether an existing page already covers the topic (use Section 7). If it does, change the status to MERGE and report it.
2. **Run a SERP check** for the primary keyword and the two best supporting keywords. Record the top 5 results, their format (listicle, guide, tool page), and the questions in "People also ask."
3. **Identify the answer format** that wins. If results are mostly listicles, write a comparison or roundup. If they are mostly guides, write a how-to.
4. **Gather 2 to 4 authoritative sources** for any factual claim. Prefer FSSAI, FDA, NABH, government, standards bodies, and original company documentation. Do not cite forums or unverified blogs for regulations.
5. **Verify every Audiment product claim** against the site or the human. If a claim is not confirmed, remove it or mark it as a question for the human.
6. **For comparisons,** collect each competitor's current pricing page and feature page, with the date checked. Do not state competitor prices without a date and source.
7. **Record findings** in the register notes column: sources used, claims needing confirmation, and any overlap found.

## 6. Writing rules

### Structure

- **Title tag:** primary keyword near the front, under about 60 characters. Use the register title if it fits; otherwise shorten it while keeping the primary keyword.
- **Meta description:** 150 to 160 characters, includes the primary keyword, states the answer or benefit, no clickbait.
- **H1:** the register title.
- **Opening (first 100 words):** the primary keyword in the first sentence, followed by a direct answer.
- **Question-form H2s** that mirror real prompts. Under each, give a 40 to 60 word direct answer before expanding.
- **Body:** Use supporting keywords once each in H2s, H3s, or body text. No keyword stuffing.
- **Length:** how-to and guides 1,500 to 2,500 words; comparisons and buyer's guides 2,000 to 3,000 words; definitions 900 to 1,500 words. Do not pad.
- **FAQ:** 3 to 5 questions at the end, each with a 2 to 3 sentence answer. Use real phrasing from the register or search data.
- **Tables** for comparisons, checklists, and timelines.
- **Images:** one relevant image or diagram with descriptive alt text. Do not use customer logos without permission.

### Tone and style

- Plain, direct, operator-to-operator. Assume the reader runs outlets and has limited time.
- Use British spelling to match the site (digitise, standardise, organisation, customise).
- Use Indian context where relevant (FSSAI, GST, regional operations) without overdoing it.
- Use the entity name "Audiment" consistently. Use the one-line description from Section 2 in the first mention.
- Avoid filler phrases such as "in today's fast-paced world," "game-changer," "unlock," and "seamless." Avoid the AI-sounding patterns the site's style notes warn against: over-formatting, generic summaries, hollow lists of three.

### Claims

- Every number needs a source, a date, or a clear label as an example or assumption.
- Do not invent statistics, customer results, or regulatory requirements.
- Do not say Audiment does something unless Section 2 lists it or the human has confirmed it.
- For regulated topics (FSSAI, NABH, drug licences, biomedical waste, GMP), cite the regulation and add "verify current requirements with the regulator or a licensed consultant."
- For comparisons, describe competitors fairly and link to their own sites rather than summarising them from memory.

### Audiment mentions

- Each post should include one natural mention of Audiment, usually in a section titled as a question such as "How Audiment handles this," plus a call to action such as "Book a 20-minute walkthrough" linking to the demo page.
- Comparison and buyer's-guide posts may include a short Audiment section, but must also cover what competitors do better.
- Do not force Audiment into posts where it doesn't fit (for example, the "scale an audit business" post or the HOLD verticals).

### AEO (answer engine optimisation)

- Answer the question in the first 60 words after each H2.
- Use lists and tables where they make the answer easier to extract.
- Name specific numbers, steps, and criteria, not general advice.
- Keep definitions crisp: "X is [category] that [what it does] for [who]."
- Add a short "Key takeaways" block of 3 to 4 sentences near the top for skimmers and AI systems.

## 8. Publishing protocol

### Front matter (every post)

```yaml
title: "[Register title]"
slug: "[Register slug]"
meta_description: "[150 to 160 characters, includes primary keyword]"
primary_keyword: "[from register]"
secondary_keywords: ["[supporting 1]", "[supporting 2]"]
author: "[Named author with ops background; to be provided by human]"
published: "[date, or leave blank for draft]"
last_updated: "[month year]"
status: "draft"            # draft → review → published
register_status: "READY"   # READY, VERIFY, MERGE, HOLD, LANDING
canonical: "https://audiment.in/blog/[slug]"
schema: ["Article", "FAQPage"]
internal_links: ["[hub url]", "[supporting url 1]", "[supporting url 2]"]
cta: "Book a 20-minute walkthrough" 
```

### Internal linking

- Each new post links to its hub and two or three supporting pages, using the supporting keyword as anchor text.
- Link from at least one existing hub page to each new post, placed inside the body text near the relevant section. Do not use footer dumps.
- Link targets must return HTTP 200 on the live site before publishing.

### Schema

- Article schema on every post.
- SoftwareApplication schema on product, solution, and comparison pages, if not already present.
- FAQ schema is optional. Google shows FAQ rich results only for a narrow set of sites, so treat it as structure, not a traffic lever.

### Pre-publish checklist (human sign-off required)

1. Primary keyword is in the URL, title tag, H1, first 100 words, one H2, and the meta description.
2. Every factual claim has a source or is clearly labelled as an example.
3. Every Audiment product claim matches Section 2 or has been confirmed.
4. No unverified prices, trial terms, customer numbers, or testimonials.
5. Comparison posts cover competitor strengths and include a "where Audiment is not the right fit" section.
6. At least one question-form H2 has a 40 to 60 word direct answer below it.
7. FAQ section present with 3 to 5 questions.
8. Internal links present, and link targets return 200.
9. Canonical tag points to the non-www URL.
10. "Last updated" date is present.
11. Image has alt text and is not a customer logo without permission.
12. No overlap with the existing URLs in Section 7, or the overlap has been resolved.

### After publishing

1. Set the page to published only after the human confirms the checklist.
2. Submit the URL for indexing in Search Console.
3. Add the new URL to the Content Clusters and SEO URL Strategy Map tabs.
4. Log the date, URL, and final status in the register.
5. Check Search Console at 28 days and 90 days. Record impressions, clicks, and average position. Flag any post with zero impressions at 90 days for review (consolidate, merge, or leave as-is).

### Autonomy limits

- The agent may draft, research, and prepare files. It must not publish without the human's checklist sign-off.
- The agent must not submit customer testimonials, invent customer names, or state prices or trial terms without confirmation.
- The agent must stop and ask if a register row is HOLD, if a claim cannot be verified, or if the target slug is already live.

## 10. Open questions for the human

These must be answered before the related posts can be written or published:

1. Is there a free trial, and if so, what are the length, limits, and whether a card is required?
2. Is pricing public or only quoted? What are the plan names and the per-outlet logic, if they can be shared?
3. Where is customer data hosted (country and provider)?
4. Does the product support multi-language interfaces? Which languages?
5. Is there a white-label or consultant option?
6. Which integrations exist today (WhatsApp, ERP, POS, Zapier, API)?
7. Which customer case studies are approved for public use, with names, logos, and figures?
8. Who is the named author for each post, and what are their credentials?
9. What CMS publishes audiment.in/blog, and what access does the agent have?
10. Is the "Book a call" flow the only conversion path, or is there a self-serve sign-up?
11. Are the free template promises (for example, the FSSAI hygiene checklist) actually available?
12. Should HOLD verticals (ICP 20 to 24) stay on hold after a review of the first 90 days of data?

## 11. Known data limits

- The Search Console data in the workbook is partial. Google hides some queries, and the historical backfill was still in progress when the workbook was exported. Use the Daily Performance tab for totals, not the sum of query rows.
- Impressions are not search volume. Position is an average and does not reflect a single rank.
- The workbook's content clusters and question maps are planning inputs, not measured demand.
- PageSpeed and Lighthouse scores are lab measurements, not field data, and fluctuate.
- Re-pull Search Console data before making any keep-or-consolidate decision.
